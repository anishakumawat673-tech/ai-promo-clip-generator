1. Problem Choice, Framing & Assumptions

Why Problem 1? Kuku’s primary growth engine is vertical microdramas (1–2 min episodes). User acquisition across TikTok, Reels, and Shorts depends on high-converting 20–60 second promo clips. Today, editors watch 50+ episodes per show manually to isolate promo moments—a 4-to-6 hour bottleneck per series. Problem 1 directly attacks this operational friction.

Core Assumptions Made (Where Brief Was Silent):

  • 3-Second Sensory Rule: Cold scrollers decide to stay or swipe within 3.0s. The opening must have immediate visceral drama (slap, gasp, accusation, drawn blade) rather than establishing scenery.
  • Dialogue Boundary Snapping: Clips cannot cut mid-sentence or mid-breath. Cuts snap deterministically to dialogue or action boundaries.
  • Standalone Coherence Priority: A promo fails if it requires prior episode lore. Universal archetypes (betrayal at the altar, cruel mother-in-law, identity reveals) rank higher than mid-season politics.

2. System Architecture: Balancing Code vs. AI

Passing 100 minutes of screenplay text directly into an LLM is slow, cost-inefficient, and prone to timestamp hallucinations. We built a hybrid two-tier pipeline:

⚙️ Deterministic Code (Python Engine)

  • Regex AST Parser: Extracts scenes, character emotions, actions, and millisecond timestamps into typed dataclasses in 0.07s for 50 episodes.
  • Sliding Candidate Generator: Generates valid windows strictly bound between 20.0s and 60.0s snapping to natural pauses.
  • Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS): Suppresses temporal overlaps (IoU > 0.50), eliminating duplicate cuts of the same scene.
  • Multi-Segment Stitching: Supports cross-scene or episode-boundary transition clips as permitted by the brief.

🤖 AI & Semantic Reasoning (LLM / Heuristics)

  • 3s Hook Scoring (0–100): Evaluates immediate scroll-stopping power and sensory shock.
  • Cliffhanger Cut Scoring (0–100): Measures unresolved tension compelling scrollers to install the app.
  • Context Clarity Scoring (0–100): Verifies self-contained intelligibility for cold viewers.
  • Ad Conversion Pitch: Generates the 1-line rationale explaining why the clip converts scrollers.

Model-Agnostic Backend: The architecture runs instantly offline via high-fidelity local heuristics (zero API cost) and connects seamlessly via a single parameter to Google Gemini Flash, OpenAI GPT-4o-mini, or custom endpoints during live interviews.

4. Quality Checking: Where Does the Tool Pick Badly?

Automated text analysis encounters predictable failure modes. We audited all 150 extracted promos and classified 4 systemic edge cases:

Failure Mode Root Cause in Screenplay Real Dataset Example Editorial Mitigation
FM1: Late-Show Lore Bleed High cliffhanger score in late episodes (Ep 35+) that requires deep backstory a cold TikTok scroller doesn't know. Ep 46 (00:17–00:47): High Priest decree on vagrant marriage legality. High drama, but cold viewers lack context. Restrict Tier-1 ad budget to Episodes 1–10, or insert a 2-second on-screen context overlay card.
FM2: Vertical Pacing Lag Clips > 40s with sparse dialogue relying on silent staring. Works on TV, but causes instant swipe-away on vertical feeds. Ep 21 (00:00–00:40): Atmospheric barn scene with only 3 dialogue lines across 40s. Automated rule penalizes dialogue turn density < 0.15 turns/sec; editor trims silent pauses.
FM3: Subtle Opening Setup Clip starts on polite conversation 4 seconds before the conflict hits, missing the critical 3-second hook window. Ep 1 (00:11–00:43): Juliana enters cathedral; reveal happens at 00:18. Opening 3s is just church doors. Snip opening 4 seconds; start directly in medias res on the Matriarch's confrontation line.
FM4: Physical/Lexical Disconnect High-stakes dialogue in script that was staged with deadpan or humorous acting in video production. Sarcastic lines ("You're a genius") misclassified as serious dramatic tension. Human editor verification dashboard with 1-click Accept/Reject before pushing to ad managers.

Human-in-the-Loop Workflow: The platform does not replace editors; it serves as an editorial co-pilot. Instead of watching 100 minutes of video, an editor reviews pre-ranked candidates with pre-filled timestamps and rationale, making 1-click Accept/Reject/Flag decisions in under 5 minutes.

5. Production Economics & Scaling to Every Show

⚡ Processing Speed & Throughput

  • 50 Episodes Parsed & Scored: 2.28 seconds on standard CPU.
  • 100-Episode Season: ~4.5 seconds execution time.
  • Zero GPU Overhead: Pure Python parsing; easily containerized on serverless workers.

💰 API Cost Efficiency (Gemini Flash / GPT-4o-mini)

  • Candidate Windowing: NMS reduces raw token load by 95% (evaluates ~150 candidates vs 3,700 blocks).
  • Tokens per Series: ~45,000 tokens total.
  • Cost per Show: < $0.005 (half a cent). 100 shows/year = $0.50 total.

6. AI Tooling Transparency & Where AI Got It Wrong

As required by the assignment brief, we document our use of AI tools during development:

  • Tools Used: Antigravity / Gemini for code scaffolding, heuristic design, and candidate prompt structuring.
  • Where AI Got It Wrong: When asked to generate promo candidates, AI initially suggested sliding windows based on raw token lengths. This caused timestamps to cut mid-sentence and hallucinated non-existent scene breaks. We overrode this by building a deterministic AST parser that binds cuts strictly to screenplay dialogue timestamps.
